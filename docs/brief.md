# Brief · Maison Vauclair

> Brief sent by CS Digital on 16 October 2026 for the role-play exercise of 21 October 2026. This is a translation: the original, reproduced as is, is in [brief.fr.md](brief.fr.md).

## The client

Maison Vauclair (fictitious company) is a family-run cheese shop and delicatessen from the canton of Vaud, with 24 employees. It sells through two shops, a WooCommerce online shop, and B2B customers (restaurants and hotels).

## The situation

- The online shop accounts for 18% of revenue, almost half of which is made at Christmas.
- Most online customers order only once a year, and the company only writes to them for the Christmas newsletter.
- B2B orders arrive by phone and email, then are keyed in again by hand.
- Fresh products only ship from Monday to Wednesday, and some orders placed at the end of the week arrive too late.
- The website is in French only, although one visitor in five speaks German.
- Gift boxes, the most profitable product, can only be put together in the shops.
- Management has no consolidated view of sales across the shops, the website and B2B, and follows its figures in several Excel files.

Management wants online revenue to grow from CHF 1.1 to 1.6 million in two years, and B2B to grow by 25% without hiring administrative staff. The marketing manager is asking for a chatbot and a mobile app. The logistics manager does not want one more tool to manage. Nobody in-house has technical skills.

## Your mission

Choose one of Maison Vauclair's problems and build a standalone mini-application that addresses it, with the technology and tools of your choice, AI included. However, for reasons of cost and control over its data, management does not want the application itself to rely on an AI model. It does not need to be connected to the existing website, and you may create the data you need. Management wants to see a first version before deciding whether to go further.

## To present at the interview

- Your work on the mini-application, in the form of your choice (online demo, code repository, mock-up).
- A one-page note for the director, who is not a technical person.
- The exports of your conversations with the AI.

The work you produce remains your property.

## What we will look at

With AI, the technical part has become the most accessible part of a developer's work. We will pay attention to it, but we will mostly look at what makes the difference beyond it:

- The technical quality of what you built.
- Visual consistency and finish: a polished, consistent and pleasant interface, and development carried through to the details.
- The thinking behind your choice, which is the most important point: how you identified a real problem at Maison Vauclair, ideally a problem the client had not thought of, and how your mini-application addresses it.

## The interview

About one hour. You first present your work in 15 to 20 minutes to non-technical people, as you would to Maison Vauclair's management: they must be able to picture themselves using your application through its interface. The technical discussion only comes afterwards, during the questions.
